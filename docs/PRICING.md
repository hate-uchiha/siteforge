# Pricing: what to charge and why

## The ladder

| Package | Price | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | $300 to $500 | One-page site, phone and map, their services, a working enquiry form, live on their domain |
| Standard | $700 to $1,200 | Everything above plus gallery, reviews, FAQ, service area pages, and a proper Google profile cleanup |
| Growth | $1,500 to $3,000 | Everything above plus 5 to 8 pages, online booking or quote flow, review request automation, and local SEO groundwork |
| Care plan | $30 to $60 per month | Hosting, backups, unlimited small text and photo edits, uptime check, monthly report |
| Local SEO retainer | $150 to $400 per month | Google Business Profile management, review generation, monthly content, citation cleanup |

## The care plan is the actual business

A one-off build pays once. Twenty care plans at $40 is **$800 every month** for maybe two hours
of work. That is the number worth chasing, and it is why you should quote the build low enough
to be an easy yes, then make the monthly the default part of the offer.

Two rules that keep it painless:

1. **Never take the domain hostage.** Register it in the client's name, or transfer it on
   request. Holding a client's domain to force a monthly payment is how you get a bad
   reputation locally, and locally is where all your work comes from.
2. **Say what the monthly covers in one sentence.** "Hosting, and I make any small change you
   text me, usually the same day." Vague retainers get cancelled because the client forgets
   what they are paying for.

## Getting paid

- **50% deposit, 50% on approval.** For local trades, one third up front and the rest on
  delivery also works.
- **Get the deposit before you build anything custom.** The demo is free because it is cheap
  for you to make. Custom work after they say yes is not.
- **Care plan by monthly card or standing order**, never by invoice they might sit on.
- **A simple written scope.** One page: what is included, what is not, the price, the
  payment terms, and the date the site goes live. Both names on it. That is enough protection
  for jobs this size.

## Do not undercharge

A $50 website gets you clients who expect $50 of attention forever, and a referral network of
people who think websites cost $50. If a price feels embarrassing to say out loud, it is too low.

A useful floor: **charge at least your real time.** A build takes about two hours of your work
once the preset matches the trade. At $400 that is a real rate. At $80 it is not worth the
follow-up conversations.

## Add-ons worth charging for

- Extra pages: $80 to $150 each
- Logo or simple brand tidy-up: $150 to $300
- Online booking integration: $250 to $500
- Photo shoot, if you can shoot: $200 to $400
- Google Business Profile setup and cleanup: $150 to $250 one-off
- Copywriting for a trade that writes badly (most of them): $150 to $300

## Never promise

- Page one of Google
- A number of new customers or leads
- A revenue figure
- That the site will "pay for itself"

Promise what you control: it will be fast, it will be correct on a phone, it will be found by
the people already looking for them, and you will pick up the phone when they call you. That is
already more than most of their competitors offer.

## Tax and admin, briefly

Keep the money separate from day to day spending from the first payment. Set aside a quarter of
what you receive for tax unless you already know your rate. Write down every job, the date, the
amount, and what is still owed. One spreadsheet is enough until you are past five jobs a month.

If you take more than a few hundred a month, register as self-employed properly in your
jurisdiction. It costs little and it makes you look like a business instead of a favour.
